# How Companies Work — Finance and Accounting by Size

## The Core Idea

Every company that makes money and spends money needs someone to track it. That is the finance function. How big that function is, who does it, and what tools they use depends entirely on how big the company is.

As a company grows, the finance function grows with it — from the owner doing it themselves in a notebook, to a full department of 50 people with a CFO, a tax team, a treasury team, and an audit committee.

---

## Small Companies (1–20 employees | R0 – R10M revenue)

### Who Does the Finance Work

There is usually no dedicated finance person. The owner does it, or a part-time bookkeeper comes in once a week. An external accounting firm handles everything that requires real skill — VAT, tax, payroll, year-end financial statements.

### The Internal Reality

- One business bank account, sometimes two
- Invoices created in Xero, Sage One, QuickBooks, or sometimes Word
- Expenses paid from the business account, sometimes the owner's personal card
- The owner has a rough sense of how much money is in the account but rarely knows their actual profit
- Payroll is simple — maybe 3 to 10 staff, done by the external accountant or a basic payroll tool

### What the External Accountant Does for Them

- Captures transactions monthly (or the owner sends a shoebox of receipts)
- Submits VAT returns on the 25th
- Runs payroll and submits PAYE on the 7th
- Produces annual financial statements at year end
- Submits the income tax return
- The owner sees their financials once a year and does not always understand them

### The Pain at This Size

The owner has no real-time view of their business. They find out they are in trouble when the bank account is nearly empty. They rely entirely on their accountant for financial information, but they only speak to their accountant once a month at best.

**Who sells to them:** You sell to their accountant, not to them directly. The accountant is the gateway.

### Software Used

| Purpose | Tools |
|---------|-------|
| Accounting | Xero, Sage One, QuickBooks, Wave |
| Invoicing | Same as above, sometimes Excel |
| Payroll | SimplePay, PaySpace, Sage Payroll |
| Banking | FNB, Nedbank, ABSA, Standard Bank — online banking |
| Everything else | Excel, WhatsApp, email |

## Medium Companies (20–200 employees | R10M – R500M revenue)

### Who Does the Finance Work

Now there is an internal finance team. At the smaller end of this range — a bookkeeper and a finance manager. At the larger end — a full team of 5 to 15 people with distinct roles.

### The Finance Team Structure

| Role | What They Do |
|------|-------------|
| **Bookkeeper** | Captures all transactions daily — invoices in, invoices out, expenses, payments |
| **Accounts Payable (AP) Clerk** | Processes supplier invoices, manages payment runs, reconciles creditors |
| **Accounts Receivable (AR) Clerk** | Invoices customers, follows up on outstanding payments, manages debtors |
| **Payroll Administrator** | Runs monthly payroll, submits PAYE/UIF/SDL, manages third-party payments |
| **Management Accountant** | Produces monthly management accounts, budget vs actuals, cost analysis |
| **Financial Manager** | Oversees the team, ensures compliance, prepares for audit, reports to the CFO or MD |
| **CFO / Finance Director** | Strategic financial leadership — fundraising, board reporting, investor relations, major financial decisions |

Not every medium company has all of these. A R50M company might have a bookkeeper, a finance manager, and outsource the rest. A R300M company will have most of these roles in-house.

### The Monthly Finance Cycle

This is what happens inside a medium company every month, in order:

**Day 1–7:**
- PAYE, UIF, and SDL submitted and paid to SARS (deadline: 7th)
- Payroll for the month is processed and salaries paid
- Pension fund contributions paid to the fund
- Medical aid contributions paid to the scheme

**Day 1–15:**
- Accounts payable team processes all supplier invoices received in the previous month
- Payment run sent — all approved supplier invoices paid
- Accounts receivable team sends statements to customers with outstanding balances
- Follow-up calls and emails to debtors who are overdue

**Day 15–25:**
- VAT return prepared from the accounting records
- VAT submitted and paid to SARS (deadline: 25th)
- Bank reconciliation completed — every transaction in the bank statement matched to a record in the accounting system

**Day 25–End of month:**
- Management accounts prepared — income statement, balance sheet, cash flow
- Budget vs actuals analysis — what did we plan to spend vs what did we actually spend, and why is there a difference
- Management accounts presented to the MD or board

**This entire cycle repeats every single month without exception.**

### Annual Events On Top of That

| Event | When | Who Does It |
|-------|------|-------------|
| Year-end audit | After financial year end | External auditors (Grant Thornton, BDO, smaller firms) |
| Annual financial statements | After audit | Signed off by directors |
| Income tax return | Within 12 months of year end | Internal tax person or external |
| CIPC annual return | On company anniversary date | Company secretary or accountant |
| EMP501 submission | February each year | Payroll administrator |
| Employment equity report | October each year | HR / payroll |
| Provisional tax | August and February | Tax person |

### The Pain at This Size

The finance team exists but is overwhelmed. They spend most of their time on the compliance cycle — VAT, payroll, month-end — and have almost no time for real analysis. Management accounts are produced but often take 10 to 15 days after month end, by which time the information is stale. Everything that is not covered by the accounting software lives in Excel.

**Who sells to them:** You sell directly to the Financial Manager or CFO. They are the decision maker. They understand the pain immediately because they live it.

### Software Used

| Purpose | Tools |
|---------|-------|
| Accounting / ERP | Sage 300, Syspro, Pastel Evolution, SAPS Business One |
| Payroll | PaySpace, Sage Payroll, SimplePay |
| Budgeting | Excel (almost always) |
| Management reporting | Excel, sometimes Power BI |
| AP/AR | Built into the accounting system |
| Banking | Business online banking — still logging in per bank |
| Document management | SharePoint, shared drives, email |
| Everything that falls through the cracks | Excel |

## Large Companies (200+ employees | R500M+ revenue)

### Who Does the Finance Work

A full finance department — anywhere from 20 to 200 people depending on complexity. Clear specialisation. Multiple layers of management.

### The Finance Department Structure

```
CFO
├── Financial Director / Deputy CFO
│
├── Financial Accounting
│   ├── General Ledger team (owns the books)
│   ├── Accounts Payable team
│   ├── Accounts Receivable team
│   └── Fixed Assets team
│
├── Management Accounting / FP&A
│   ├── Budgeting and forecasting
│   ├── Management reporting
│   └── Business partnering (finance person embedded in each business unit)
│
├── Treasury
│   ├── Cash management
│   ├── Banking relationships
│   ├── FX management (forward contracts, hedging)
│   └── Debt management
│
├── Tax
│   ├── VAT compliance
│   ├── Income tax
│   ├── Transfer pricing
│   └── SARS dispute management
│
├── Payroll
│   ├── Payroll processing
│   ├── Third-party payments
│   └── SARS submissions
│
└── Internal Audit
    └── Reviews controls and compliance internally
```

### The Key Difference at This Size

At this level, finance is not just about keeping records — it is about driving the business. The CFO is in the boardroom. The FP&A team is modelling scenarios for the CEO. The treasury team is managing hundreds of millions in cash across multiple currencies.

But the fundamental problems are bigger, not smaller. More entities to consolidate. More bank accounts to reconcile. More intercompany transactions to match. More regulatory filings across more jurisdictions. More people, more complexity, more that can go wrong.

### What Large Companies Spend on Finance Software

These companies buy enterprise software. The deals are large:

| System | Purpose | Cost (rough) |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| SAP S/4HANA or Oracle ERP | Core accounting and operations | R5M–R50M+ implementation |
| Hyperion or Anaplan | FP&A, budgeting, planning | R2M–R10M/year |
| Kyriba or FIS Quantum | Treasury management | R1M–R5M/year |
| Workday | HR and payroll | R2M–R10M/year |
| Power BI or Tableau | Business intelligence | R500K–R2M/year |
| Big 4 audit fees | External audit | R2M–R10M/year |

Even at this scale — Excel is still everywhere. Finance people at SAP-using companies still run important analysis in Excel. The enterprise systems are the system of record. Excel is where thinking happens.

### Who Sells to Them

Nobody sells to them quickly. Procurement processes take 6 to 12 months. The CFO has a budget cycle. IT has a security review. Legal has a contract review. Procurement negotiates the price. This is not where you start.

## The Finance Cycle — Visual Summary

```
DAILY
─────
Bank transactions arrive → reconciled against records
Supplier invoices arrive → captured and approved
Customer payments received → matched against invoices
Debtors chased for overdue payments

WEEKLY
──────
Payment run → approved supplier invoices paid
Cash position reviewed → how much do we have, how much is coming in/going out
Debtors report → who owes us, how overdue

MONTHLY
───────
1st–7th:   Payroll processed → salaries paid → PAYE/UIF/SDL submitted to SARS
1st–20th:  All prior month invoices captured, reconciled, bank statement matched
20th–25th: VAT return prepared and submitted to SARS
25th–end:  Management accounts produced → income statement, balance sheet, cash flow
End:       Budget vs actuals analysis → report to management/board

QUARTERLY
─────────
Provisional tax calculated and paid
Forecast updated
Board pack produced
(For financial services: FSCA regulatory returns)

ANNUALLY
────────
Year-end audit
Annual financial statements
Income tax return
EMP501 (payroll reconciliation)
CIPC annual return
Employment equity report
```

## What This Means for Building Finance Products

### Small companies — sell through their accountant
They do not have the internal capability to evaluate or implement your product. Their accountant is the gatekeeper. Win the accountant, reach the client.

### Medium companies — sell directly to the Financial Manager or CFO
They have the pain, the budget, and the authority to say yes. The conversation is about time and money. How many hours does this save? What does that cost in staff time? The ROI math is fast.

### Large companies — do not start here
Long sales cycles, complex procurement, competing internal projects, existing enterprise vendors with long contracts. The money is large but the path is slow. Build your reputation with medium companies first. Large companies will come when you have proof.

## The One Thing That Is True at Every Size

Every company — from a 3-person startup to a 50,000-person conglomerate — has the same fundamental finance workflow:

1. Money comes in
2. Money goes out
3. Someone records it
4. Someone reports on it
5. Someone files it with SARS

The tools, the team, and the complexity scale. The workflow does not change.

Every problem worth solving in finance is somewhere in steps 1 through 5. The opportunity is always in making one of those steps faster, more accurate, or less painful than it currently is.
