# Unsolved Finance Problems — Co-Founder's Map

## How to Use This Document

These are real problems in finance and accounting that are either unsolved or poorly solved, specifically in the South African context. For each one: the problem, who has it, why it has not been solved yet, and whether it is a good fit for where you are right now.

The problems are organized from most accessible to hardest to enter. Read all of them. Then pick the conversation you want to have next.

## Problem 1 — Cash Visibility Across Multiple Banks

**What the problem is:**
Any company with more than one bank account — which is most medium-to-large businesses — has no single view of how much cash they have right now. The CFO logs into Nedbank, then FNB, then ABSA, downloads each statement separately, opens Excel, pastes everything together, and calculates the total cash position manually. This happens every morning.

For companies with operations across multiple African countries — accounts in Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, Zambia — it is even worse. Each country is a different bank, different currency, different login, different format.

**Who has this problem:**
- Any company with R10M+ in annual revenue
- Subsidiaries of larger groups
- Companies with multi-country African operations (very common in SA — Shoprite, Tiger Brands, construction companies, logistics firms all operate across Africa)

**Why it has not been solved:**
SA banks have terrible APIs. Nedbank, FNB, ABSA, Standard Bank — most do not have open banking APIs available to third parties. Connecting directly to bank accounts is technically hard in SA. The workaround is parsing bank statement files (OFX, CSV, PDF) that banks provide.

**Why it is solvable now:**
Open banking regulation is coming to SA (the Competition Commission has been pushing for it). Early movers who build the file-parsing approach today will be positioned when bank APIs open up. Also, international banks operating in Africa (Citi, Standard Chartered, HSBC) DO have APIs for their corporate clients.

**The product:** A single dashboard showing cash across all accounts, all banks, all currencies, updated daily. CFO opens one screen instead of logging into seven portals.

**Fit for you:** High. No deep finance knowledge required — it is a data aggregation and display problem. The sales conversation is simple: "How do you know how much cash you have across all your accounts right now?"

## Problem 2 — Intercompany Reconciliation

**What the problem is:**
Large South African companies are often groups — a holding company with many subsidiaries that buy from and sell to each other. At month end, every intercompany transaction must cancel out. If Company A says it paid Company B R5 million, Company B must show it received R5 million. If they don't match — something is wrong and someone has to find it.

This reconciliation is done in Excel, manually, every single month, across potentially hundreds of transaction pairs. At large SA conglomerates like Remgro, Naspers, or any mining group — this is a massive monthly exercise involving entire teams.

**Who has this problem:**
- Any business group with more than 2 entities
- SA conglomerates and their subsidiaries
- Any holding company structure
- Property groups with multiple SPVs (special purpose vehicles)

**Who specifically to talk to:** Group Financial Controllers, Group CFOs, Management Accountants at holding companies

**Why it has not been solved:**
The existing solutions (SAP Intercompany, Oracle) are enterprise-level and cost millions. Mid-size groups — 5 to 50 entities — are too big for manual Excel but too small for SAP. That gap is wide open.

**The product:** Upload transactions from both sides. System matches them automatically. Flags the ones that don't match with a specific explanation. All within one platform instead of emailing Excel files between subsidiaries.

**Connection to your existing research:** This is exactly what your friend described — downloading files from different systems and comparing manually. Same problem, different context. The reconciliation engine you would build for fund operations is the same engine here.

**Fit for you:** Very high. Directly connected to the reconciliation work you have already researched.

## Problem 3 — SARS Dispute and Correspondence Management

**What the problem is:**
SARS has become significantly more aggressive with compliance enforcement. They issue:
- Additional assessments (they say you owe more tax than you submitted)
- Requests for relevant material (they want supporting documents within 21 days)
- Verification letters (they want to verify your VAT return before paying a refund)
- Audit notifications
- Penalty notices

Every one of these has a strict deadline — typically 30 days to respond. Missing the deadline means the assessment becomes final, even if it is wrong.

Accounting firms and tax departments manage these disputes in their Outlook inbox and a spreadsheet. There is no dedicated system. Disputes get missed. Deadlines are tracked in someone's head. Documents are scattered across email threads.

**Who has this problem:**
- Every accounting firm (they handle disputes on behalf of clients)
- Corporate tax departments at large companies
- Tax practitioners — specialists who only handle SARS disputes

**The scale of the problem:** SARS issued millions of additional assessments in the last 3 years. Every single one of those needed a response managed by someone using Excel and email.

**Why it has not been solved:**
This is a very South Africa-specific problem. International tax software does not know about SARS eFiling, South African tax legislation, or the specific SARS dispute process. The only tools that exist are generic case management systems adapted poorly for this use.

**The product:** A dedicated SARS dispute management system. Every dispute is logged — taxpayer name, dispute type, assessment amount, deadline, status, documents. Automated reminders before deadlines. Document storage linked to each dispute. Integration with SARS eFiling for submission tracking.

**Connection to your existing research:** You already have the accounting firm relationship and research. This is an add-on product for the same customers — accounting firms who are already managing SARS disputes manually.

**Fit for you:** Very high. Same buyer as the accounting firm product. The conversation is already half open.

## Problem 4 — Multi-Entity Consolidation for SME Groups

**What the problem is:**
Many South African entrepreneurs own multiple businesses — a trading company, a property holding company, a management company, maybe a trust. Their accountant sees each entity separately. Nobody has a consolidated view of the owner's total financial picture.

At year end the accountant tries to consolidate everything manually in Excel — eliminate intercompany transactions, combine balance sheets, calculate the group's true net worth. This takes weeks and costs significant fees. During the year, the owner has no idea what their consolidated position is.

**Who has this problem:**
- Any entrepreneur with more than one registered entity (very common in SA)
- Family businesses with multiple operating companies
- Property investors with multiple SPVs
- Business owners with a trading company and a property portfolio

**The market size:** There are over 700,000 registered private companies in South Africa. A significant portion of business owners own more than one. This is an enormous market.

**Why it has not been solved:**
Accounting software (Xero, Sage) is entity-by-entity. Consolidation features exist in enterprise software (Caseware) but it is complex and expensive. Nothing simple and affordable exists for the owner who just wants to see: combined revenue, combined expenses, combined debt, combined net worth — across all their entities — in real time.

**The product:** Connect to each entity's Xero/Sage account. Pull data automatically. Show the owner a consolidated dashboard — group income statement, group balance sheet, group cash position. Updated monthly when accounts are done.

**Who to talk to:** Accountants who serve business owners with multiple entities. Business owners themselves. Wealth managers who advise these clients.

**Fit for you:** High. The data connection work is the same as what you're building for fund operations. Different display, same pipeline.

## Problem 5 — BBBEE Compliance Tracking

**What the problem is:**
Every South African company that does business with government or large corporates needs a valid BBBEE (Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment) certificate. The score is calculated across 5 elements:
- Ownership
- Management control
- Skills development
- Enterprise and supplier development
- Socio-economic development

Companies calculate their score annually for verification by an accredited ratings agency. The problem: the data needed for the calculation is scattered across HR systems (for management control and skills development), financial records (for supplier spend), and ownership documents (share registers). Companies scramble to collect this data every year, often realize at verification that their score is lower than expected, and have no way to monitor it between verifications.

**Who has this problem:**
- Every company that tenders for government contracts
- Every company that supplies to large corporates (who check BBBEE as part of procurement)
- Practically every medium-to-large South African business

**The product:** A system that connects to HR, payroll, and accounting data and calculates the BBBEE score continuously throughout the year. Shows where the score will land. Alerts when a specific element is dropping. Helps plan interventions before verification.

**Why this is interesting:** BBBEE is purely South African. No international software solves this properly. The existing players are small, manual, and use Excel. The regulations change regularly, creating ongoing value in a maintained compliance tool.

**Fit for you:** Medium. Requires understanding of the BBBEE codes, which is a learning curve. But a single conversation with a BBBEE verification agency would give you everything you need to know.

## Problem 6 — Crypto Tax and CGT Tracking

**What the problem is:**
South Africa has a large and growing crypto investor population. SARS now explicitly requires disclosure of crypto gains and losses on tax returns. The problem: crypto investors make hundreds or thousands of small transactions — trades, DeFi interactions, staking rewards, airdrops. Calculating the capital gains tax on all of these is extremely complex.

The specific SA complications:
- Different treatment for traders (income tax) vs investors (CGT)
- Eighth Schedule (CGT) calculations require knowing the base cost of each coin at acquisition
- SARS allows several methods of determining base cost — first-in-first-out, weighted average
- Foreign exchange — if you hold USD-denominated assets, there is an FX gain/loss too

Tax practitioners are struggling because they have no tool that understands South African tax law AND handles crypto properly. Most crypto tax tools are US-built and use US tax rules.

**Who has this problem:**
- Crypto investors who need to do their tax returns
- Tax practitioners whose clients have crypto (increasingly everyone)
- High-net-worth individuals with significant crypto portfolios

**The product:** Connect to South African crypto exchanges (Luno, VALR, AltCoinTrader — all SA-based), pull all transaction history, calculate CGT and income tax implications using SA tax rules, produce a schedule that can be handed to a tax practitioner or submitted directly.

**Why now:** SARS enforcement is ramping up. People who ignored crypto on their returns for 3 years are starting to worry. The urgency is there.

**Fit for you:** Medium. High interest from a consumer perspective. B2B angle is selling to tax practitioners as a tool they use for all their crypto clients.

## Problem 7 — Payroll and HR Compliance Across Africa

**What the problem is:**
South African companies expanding into other African countries face a nightmare. Every African country has:
- Different income tax structures and rates
- Different social security contributions (pension funds, provident funds, NHIF, NSSF equivalents)
- Different employment law (notice periods, severance, leave entitlements)
- Different payslip requirements
- Different submission deadlines to tax authorities

A SA company operating in SA, Kenya, Nigeria, and Zimbabwe has to comply with 4 completely different payroll regimes, often using 4 different local payroll providers, none of which talk to each other. The Group HR Manager has no consolidated view of total payroll cost across the group.

**Who has this problem:**
- SA companies with African expansion (construction, retail, logistics, telecoms)
- Pan-African businesses headquartered in SA
- International companies using SA as their Africa hub

**The product:** A multi-country African payroll compliance platform. Not a payroll processing tool — a compliance management tool. Each country's rules are encoded. Managers input the salary data; the system calculates the correct deductions for each country and generates the correct submission forms for each tax authority.

**Why this is not solved:** Africa is too fragmented for international players (ADP, Workday) to build country-specific compliance for 54 countries profitably. Local players solve one country. Nobody solves the multi-country view.

**Fit for you:** Lower right now — requires significant knowledge of multiple African tax regimes. But the market is large and the problem is real. Better as a Year 2 product after you have built trust in the SA market.

## Problem 8 — Accounts Payable Fraud and Duplicate Payments

**What the problem is:**
South African businesses lose significant money every year to:
- Duplicate invoices — same invoice submitted twice, paid twice
- Vendor fraud — employees setting up fake suppliers
- Invoice manipulation — legitimate invoices with amounts changed
- Ghost employees — people on the payroll who do not exist

Most companies have weak controls because their accounts payable process is: invoice arrives → someone approves it in email → it gets captured into accounting software → payment runs. No systematic check for duplicates. No systematic fraud detection.

**Who has this problem:**
- Every company that pays suppliers (all of them)
- Particularly high-risk: companies with high transaction volumes, government departments, construction companies, healthcare

**Why it is solvable:**
Every invoice has patterns — same supplier, same amount, similar dates. Duplicate detection is a straightforward matching problem with AI assistance. You have already designed this for the accounting platform — the duplicate invoice detection feature is exactly this.

**The product:** An accounts payable screening tool. Before any payment is made, the system checks: have we paid this before? Is this supplier legitimate? Does this invoice number match a previous one? Flags anything suspicious for human review before money leaves the bank.

**Fit for you:** Very high. The technology you are building for the accounting platform (duplicate detection, anomaly flagging) is directly reusable here. Different buyer — not accountants, but CFOs and finance managers at medium-to-large companies.

## Problem 9 — Retirement Fund Administration for Smaller Funds

**What the problem is:**
South Africa has thousands of retirement funds — employer-sponsored pension and provident funds. The large ones (R10B+) use sophisticated administrators. The small ones (under R500M) use manual processes or expensive systems that were designed for large funds.

Every retirement fund must:
- Process monthly contributions from the employer
- Reconcile contributions to member accounts
- Calculate investment returns per member
- Handle withdrawals, retirements, and death benefits
- Produce member benefit statements annually
- Submit returns to the FSCA

Small fund administrators (often accounting firms or small specialist companies) do most of this in Excel. Member records in spreadsheets. Contribution reconciliation in spreadsheets. Benefit calculations in spreadsheets.

**Who has this problem:**
- Small retirement fund administrators
- Large employers who self-administer their own fund
- Employee benefit consultants

**Fit for you:** Medium. Niche market but very high value. A single fund administrator might pay R30,000/month for a tool that properly handles this.

## The Problems to Ignore (For Now)

These are real problems but the wrong starting point:

| Problem | Why to ignore for now |
|---------|----------------------|
| Banking and lending | Regulated by the PA (Prudential Authority). Requires a banking licence or partnership with a bank. Very high barriers. |
| Insurance | Regulated by FSCA. Complex actuarial calculations. Long sales cycles. |
| Investment management (portfolio management itself) | Automating the operations around it, not the investing. Do not cross into the investing side. |
| Government / public sector finance | PFMA compliance. Political complexity. Payments take 6–12 months. Not a startup market. |
| Consumer fintech | High customer acquisition cost. Regulatory complexity (NCA, FSR Act). Very competitive. |

## The Co-Founder's Real Advice

You do not need to know these industries to find the problems. You need the right conversation.

The question that unlocks every one of these is:

> *"Walk me through your worst Monday morning. What is the thing you dread doing that you know is coming?"*

Every finance and accounting person has an answer. The answer is the product.

The people to go find:
- Group Financial Controllers at holding companies (intercompany, consolidation)
- Tax managers at medium-large companies (SARS disputes, transfer pricing)
- Retirement fund administrators (pension admin)
- CFOs of companies with African operations (cash visibility, multi-country payroll)
- BBBEE consultants and verification agencies (BBBEE compliance)
- Crypto tax practitioners (crypto CGT)

One conversation with each of these people will tell you more than any document I can write.
